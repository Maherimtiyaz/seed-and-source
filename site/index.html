Our founding story

Every Company You Admire Was Once a Seed in the Dark

A new weekly paper digs up how startups really grew, from the first idea down to the code, one company at a time.

By the Seed & Source deskRead time: 2 minutes

By the time a company makes the front page, its beginning is long out of sight. The billion-dollar round, the launch on every timeline, the product your friends suddenly use: it all looks like it happened overnight. It never does.

Every one of those companies started as a seed. A founder annoyed by a problem that wouldn't go away. A first version nobody asked for. An investor who said no. Then, quietly, roots: a database someone chose, an open-source library that saved months, a decision that would matter for years.

Most startup writing covers only what grew above ground. Funding news without the code. Engineering posts without the business. Founder legends without the numbers.

Seed & Source reports both halves. Each Tuesday this paper takes one company, from YC launches to AI startups to the open-source projects quietly becoming businesses, and follows it from seed to bloom. Every claim is linked to the place we found it.

Day 0

A1 · The Seed

It Starts Where Nobody Is Looking

An annoyed founder, a rough sketch, and usually a “no.”

A founder gets fed up with a problem that won't go away. They build a rough answer, pitch it, and are often turned down. There is no logo yet, no users, no headline. Nothing about it is visible.

By the time you read about a company, this part has been buried for years. We go back and dig it up: who was annoyed, by what, and the first version they shipped.

In every issue: The Story where the idea came from

Month 2

A2 · The Roots

Plants Grow Roots Before Anyone Sees a Leaf

Startups do too. Before the first customer, there is a stack.

Underneath every product is a set of choices: a database someone picked, an open-source library that saved six months, an engineering decision that holds the whole thing up for years.

More of it is public than ever, in open repositories, changelogs and engineering blogs. We read them, and we explain the one decision that matters most, in plain words a non-engineer can follow.

In every issue: Under the Hood the stack and the decision behind it

Month 6

B1 · The Sprout

Then, One Morning, Something Breaks the Surface

The first customers, and the moment founders remember.

A launch finally lands. The first customers arrive. Sometimes it's a new feature; surprisingly often it's a new sentence that explains the product better than the old one did.

That turning point is the moment founders tell stories about, and the one that's easiest to learn from. We find it, and we show you the proof.
